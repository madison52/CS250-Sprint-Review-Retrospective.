# CS 250 Portfolio: Sprint Review and Retrospective

This repository includes my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250, where I looked back at the SNHU Travel project through the lens of Scrum. The document pulls together the roles I played, the user stories I worked on, and what I learned about communication and change.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating "user stories" help with this?

I start by asking what the user is trying to do and why, instead of jumping to a feature. A user story keeps that in front of the team because it says who wants something, what they want, and why it matters. The acceptance criteria then turn that need into a checklist. For the Top Five Destination List, the criteria said each destination needed a name, a one sentence description, a picture, and a link to the top selling package. I built the Java list against that checklist, so I could check my own work. When a story was vague, like "customize the list for each user," I asked the Product Owner specific questions before I wrote any code.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I like building in small pieces I can show and test, then adjusting as I learn. The change from a scrollable list to a slide show, and the later shift to wellness destinations, showed me that changing direction is cheap when the work comes in small increments. In my future work, I want to keep a visible board, a short daily check-in, acceptance criteria written before I start, testers involved early, and a review at the end of each sprint.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

It means communicating clearly and early. I ask narrow questions, follow up in writing, and disagree with a reason and an alternative instead of staying quiet. It also means sharing what I know so nobody becomes a single point of failure, and noticing when a teammate is stuck so I can help.
